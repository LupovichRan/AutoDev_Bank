# Build the Open Bank on Postgres First

The best-evidenced way to build a disruptive web + mobile digital bank on free open-source infrastructure in November 2026 is to put an **append-only, double-entry ledger inside PostgreSQL** (invariants enforced in SQL, seeded from the pgledger pattern) behind a **single Go service** that uses Postgres itself as the job queue, and to treat maker-checker as a transactional workflow table guarded by a small policy engine rather than as a feature of some exotic core. The only purpose-built OSS ledgers that are both truly free and production-evidenced are TigerBeetle (Apache-2.0, Jepsen-audited) and a hand-rolled Postgres ledger; Formance v3 now needs Go 1.27 plus an embedded Raft/Pebble stack, Blnk leans on Redis locks, Midaz has quietly moved to the Elastic License, and Apache Fineract is the only turnkey maker-checker but is a large Java monolith with no production deployment guide ([TigerBeetle](https://github.com/tigerbeetle/tigerbeetle); [pgledger](https://github.com/pgr0ss/pgledger); [Midaz](https://github.com/LerianStudio/midaz); [Fineract](https://github.com/apache/fineract)). Authentication should be passkey-first with a device-bound signing key for payment SCA, delivered by a thin in-house Go service, with Keycloak reserved for staff SSO and any FAPI-grade open-banking authorization server ([Keycloak release notes](https://www.keycloak.org/docs/latest/release_notes/index.html)). The client should be a web core (Svelte 5 or Lit) inside a Capacitor shell, because a PWA alone cannot deliver hardware attestation, device-bound keys or App Store presence ([firt.dev](https://firt.dev/notes/pwa-ios/); [Apple guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)). Instant payments with Verification of Payee, passkeys, pots and outbound open-banking APIs are now table stakes; an assistant that executes actions, scoped agent mandates, EUDI-wallet onboarding, multi-bank orchestration and a publicly provable daily reconciliation are the differentiators; tokenised deposits and the digital euro remain speculative. For real production data today, **80+ UK Open Banking "Open Data" endpoints plus ECB, Bank of Israel, Bank of England, Fed and US Treasury feeds were live-verified with zero credentials**; Enable Banking's restricted production mode and Plaid's US trial are the free-signup routes to real account data; every path to a third party's customer accounts in the UK, EU or Israel requires a licence or an agent agreement with a licensee ([participant_store.json](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/OpenBankingUK/opendata-api-spec-compiled/master/participant_store.json); [Enable Banking](https://enablebanking.com/docs/api/reference/); [Herzog Fox & Neeman](https://herzoglaw.co.il/en/news-and-insights/israel-securities-authority-publishes-directive-for-payment-companies-regarding-the-implementation-of-an-open-banking-standard/)). Strategically, the evidence from Nubank, Revolut, Chime, Synapse and the £21m–£29m FCA fines says: position as an open "bank operating system" with a small licensed reference deployment, make reconciliation and financial-crime controls the headline, and never fund the model on interchange.

## An append-only Postgres ledger beats TigerBeetle for a small team

Every credible reference design converges on the same invariant set for a bank ledger: an immutable journal where every transaction balances to zero per currency, integer minor-unit amounts, client-supplied idempotency keys enforced at the storage layer, two-phase pending/posted/voided transfers with expiry, corrections by reversal rather than edit, and a separate mutable control plane for customer and product metadata. Square's Books stores only `books.balance` as mutable and fixes mistakes with correcting entries ("this is a feature"), Modern Treasury makes ledger transactions immutable once posted and recommends approving against an "available" balance that assumes outgoing funds are gone and incoming have not arrived, and TigerBeetle reduces the whole domain to "two entities (accounts and transfers) and one invariant" ([Square](https://developer.squareup.com/blog/books-an-immutable-double-entry-accounting-database-service/); [Modern Treasury](https://www.moderntreasury.com/journal/how-to-think-about-ledger-balances); [TigerBeetle](https://docs.tigerbeetle.com/concepts/debit-credit/)). The pitfalls are equally consistent: float arithmetic, read-then-update races, hot shared accounts serialising on row locks, and idempotency semantics that change with isolation level; pgledger documents that concurrent duplicate idempotent calls wait under Read Committed but fail under Repeatable Read or Serializable and must be retried ([pgledger](https://github.com/pgr0ss/pgledger); [PostgreSQL docs](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/current/transaction-iso.html)).

TigerBeetle is the strongest piece of engineering in the field. It is Apache-2.0, offers only strict serializability, processes each u128-identified transfer at most once even under client retries, stores immutable hash-chained checksummed data, and was independently audited by Jepsen in June 2025, which found it "appeared to meet its promise of Strong Serializability" as of 0.16.30 with "exceptional resilience to disk corruption" ([Safety docs](https://docs.tigerbeetle.com/concepts/safety/); [Jepsen](https://jepsen.io/analyses/tigerbeetle-0.16.11)). Super Payments moved its ledger to TigerBeetle in under three months specifically because hot merchant accounts caused row-lock bottlenecks in their relational design ([TigerBeetle story](https://tigerbeetle.com/stories/super)). But the operational bill is real: the project recommends **six replicas across three cloud providers, each on its own machine with ECC RAM and local NVMe**, the cluster size cannot change after creation, there is no authentication so it must never face untrusted clients, you lose SQL and joins for financial rows, and the docs insist on a separate general-purpose control-plane database anyway ([Deploying](https://docs.tigerbeetle.com/operating/deploying/); [Hardware](https://docs.tigerbeetle.com/operating/hardware/); [System architecture](https://docs.tigerbeetle.com/coding/system-architecture/)). The Super Payments metadata lists "100+ TPS", a reminder that most adopters run far below its capacity; the choice is driven by correctness guarantees, not throughput.

Against that, a minimal Postgres ledger is small and fast enough for any new bank. pgledger is a single SQL file with accounts, transfers and entries, and it benchmarked at **10,637 transfers/s on a laptop with vanilla Postgres 17.5**, dropping 29% when the account set shrank from 50 to 10 (hot-account contention) ([pgrs.net](https://www.pgrs.net/2025/05/16/pgledger-in-postgresql-is-fast/)). Its author added idempotency keys in October 2026 and now explicitly frames the project as "a starter kit for a ledger" to copy and adapt, noting that idempotency was "tricky to implement correctly" ([pgrs.net, Oct 2026](https://www.pgrs.net/2026/10/29/coding-agents-starter-kits-and-pgledger/)). Other Postgres-native designs show the enforcement pattern: ledger-core uses a deferrable constraint trigger so debits equal credits at commit, and the same technique plus a unique constraint on the idempotency key is recommended as the way to push invariants into the database rather than the application ([ledger-core](https://github.com/ByteEntropyDotCom/ledger-core); [dev.to](https://dev.to/tomasralmeida/why-building-a-ledger-is-harder-than-it-looks-21cm)).

The other candidates each fail a KISS or licence test. Formance Ledger is MIT and has a vendor benchmark of 106,000 tx/s, but v3 replaced PostgreSQL with an in-process Pebble LSM plus a single etcd/raft group and requires **Go 1.27+, Just and Nix** to build, so an adopter inherits a new consensus stack and cannot even compile it in this project's Go 1.24 sandbox ([Formance GitHub](https://github.com/formancehq/ledger); [Formance pricing](https://www.formance.com/pricing)). Blnk is Apache-2.0 on Postgres plus Redis, with inflight holds and an optional global hash chain, but concurrency safety rests on Redis locks taken "in a consistent order" with an optimistic version as "the database-level safety net", and RBAC and audit logs are Enterprise-only behind a licence key ([Blnk concurrency](https://docs.blnkfinance.com/guides/concurrency.md); [Blnk enterprise](https://docs.blnkfinance.com/deploy/enterprise.md)). Midaz, often listed as Apache-2.0, now describes itself as a "Source-Available Core Banking Platform" under Elastic License 2.0 with licence-key gating, a change made after v1.2.0 ([Midaz](https://github.com/LerianStudio/midaz); [pkg.go.dev v1.2.0](https://beta.pkg.go.dev/github.com/LerianStudio/midaz@v1.2.0)). Apache Fineract 1.16.1 (released 29 October 2026) is the only option shipping loans, savings, accruals, close-of-business batch, multi-tenancy and a "four-eyes" maker-checker with a Checker Inbox, but it is a 12k-commit Java/Spring Batch monolith whose README says the project "does not provide a comprehensive guide for deploying Fineract in production", and its design centre is microfinance lending rather than real-time payments ([Apache release catalog](https://release-catalog.apache.org/fineract/index.html); [Fineract GitHub](https://github.com/apache/fineract); [Mifos maker-checker](https://docs.mifos.org/mifosx/user-manual/for-administrators-mifos-x-platform/administration/system/configure-maker-checker-tasks.md)). Open Bank Project is an AGPL/commercial API layer, not a ledger, and the Moov libraries are Apache-2.0 payment-format parsers (ACH, Fedwire, ISO 20022) that sit beside a ledger rather than replacing it ([OBP](https://github.com/OpenBankProject/OBP-API/); [moov-io/ach](https://github.com/moov-io/ach)).

The recommendation is therefore an in-house ledger on PostgreSQL with three tables (accounts carrying currency, version and stored balance; transactions with a UNIQUE idempotency key; entries with directional amount and running balance), a deferrable constraint trigger enforcing zero-sum per transaction per currency, `SELECT ... FOR UPDATE` on accounts in sorted ID order, BIGINT minor units, explicit holds modelled as pending state with `expires_at`, both a system timestamp and a UTC effective date per entry, and an explicit period-close lock in the Fineract style where backdating before a closure is an error ([Mifos closing entries](https://docs.mifos.org/mifosx/user-manual/for-operational-users-mifos-x-web-app/accounting-operations/closing-entries.md); [TigerBeetle data modeling](https://docs.tigerbeetle.com/coding/data-modeling/)). Steal TigerBeetle's semantics without its cluster: client-generated transfer IDs, at-most-once by uniqueness, pending transfers that can be resolved exactly once, balance-bound flags per account, and FX as linked transfers through per-currency liquidity accounts rounded in the liquidity provider's favour ([Transfer reference](https://docs.tigerbeetle.com/reference/transfer/); [FX recipe](https://docs.tigerbeetle.com/coding/recipes/currency-exchange/)). Keep TigerBeetle as the documented "scale later" swap for the balances-and-transfers data plane if hot-account contention ever appears, which is exactly the hybrid TigerBeetle's own architecture guide describes. In the sandbox the only variable is PostgreSQL 16 rather than 18: `uuidv7()` and temporal `WITHOUT OVERLAPS` constraints are PG18 features, so generate UUIDv7 in Go and enforce effective-date overlap rules in application code until the production target is PG18 ([PostgreSQL 18 press kit](https://www.postgresql.org/about/press/presskit18/)).

## Passkeys plus a device-bound signing key satisfy SCA; Keycloak only for staff and open banking

WebAuthn Level 3 became a W3C Recommendation on 25 August 2026 and new work moves to Level 4, so the API surface is stable enough to build on; Level 3's Related Origin Requests let one passkey cover five or more brand domains ([W3C](https://www.w3.org/news/2026/web-authentication-an-api-for-accessing-public-key-credentials-level-3-is-now-a-w3c-recommendation/); [startwithidentity](https://startwithidentity.com/blog/2026-08-31-webauthn-level-3-becomes-a-w3c-recommendation/)). Passkeys are production reality at Chase, U.S. Bank, Wells Fargo, Fidelity, Ubank and Revolut, and Chinabank made them mandatory for transaction approval from March 2026, replacing SMS OTP ([Corbado](https://www.corbado.com/faq/banking-passkeys)). The regulatory catch is that the EBA has issued no passkey-specific statement; the governing text is still the 2019 opinion, under which an app, browser or key pair is a "possession" element only when it "include[s] a device-binding process that ensures a unique connection" to the device, and dynamic linking (RTS Art. 5) must bind amount and payee into the authentication code, which a bare passkey login does not do ([EBA Opinion](https://www.eba.europa.eu/sites/default/files/documents/10180/2622242/4bf4e536-69a5-44a5-a685-de42e292ef78/EBA%20Opinion%20on%20SCA%20elements%20under%20PSD2%20.pdf); [Security Boulevard](https://securityboulevard.com/2026/07/passkeys-and-psd2-sca-dynamic-linking-with-webauthn/)). FIDO itself recommends device-bound passkeys "for user accounts that require the highest level of protection" and notes synced passkeys "create a dependency on the passkey provider" ([FIDO whitepaper](https://fidoalliance.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/06/FIDO_EDWG-Spring-2023_Paper-4_-Authentication-for-Moderate-Assurance-Use-Cases_FINALv3.docx-1.pdf)). PSD3/PSR final texts were agreed in April–May 2026 with Official Journal publication expected in Q4 2026 and application not before roughly 2028, and they push SCA toward phishing resistance and extend Verification of Payee to all credit transfers ([MoFo](https://www.mofo.com/resources/insights/260430-psd3-and-the-payment-services-regulation-key-developments); [Avanto](https://avanto.team/insights/psd3-psr-status)).

The lowest-regret design follows what Fidelity, Merrill and Nuvision already run: **passkey-first login, a second registered authenticator as recovery, and step-up for sensitive actions**, with the step-up implemented as a device-bound, non-exportable key in the bank app (Secure Enclave or StrongBox Keystore) that signs the canonical payment payload so dynamic linking is satisfied ([Corbado](https://www.corbado.com/faq/banking-passkeys)). Synced passkeys are fine as the login factor paired with knowledge or inherence; they should not be the sole possession element for payments. Build this as a small in-house Go service: WebAuthn credential storage, short-lived opaque sessions, device registration with Play Integrity and App Attest verification, and a transaction-signing endpoint. Keycloak is Apache-2.0, ~37.7k stars, and since 26.4 marks FAPI 2 Final, DPoP and passkeys as "supported", with OpenID4VCI promoted to preview in 26.8 for EUDI-wallet work, but it is a 1–2 GiB Java process per node and ROPC is off by default since v25; it earns its keep for staff SSO (DORA demands strong authentication and uniquely identifiable users) and for any FAPI-conformant open-banking authorization server, not for a first-party customer channel ([Keycloak release notes](https://www.keycloak.org/docs/latest/release_notes/index.html); [ttlab deep-dive](https://ttlab.hopto.org/blog/culture/2026-05-16-keycloak-authentik-zitadel-ory-sso-oidc-saml-fapi-2026-deep-dive?lang=en)). Ory Kratos/Hydra (Apache-2.0) is the credible headless alternative if the team wants to own the login UI; Zitadel moved to AGPL-3.0 with v3 and Hanko's backend is AGPL, which is acceptable for self-hosting but a consideration for a project others may embed ([Zitadel blog](https://zitadel.com/blog/apache-to-agpl); [Hanko](https://github.com/teamhanko/hanko)).

Maker-checker is a workflow problem first and an authorization problem second. The state (initiated → pending approvals → approved/rejected/executed, with required approver count, amount tier and a `not_before` for time locks) must live transactionally next to the ledger; the policy engine only decides whether a given principal may take the next step. All five candidate engines are Apache-2.0. The Zanzibar-style engines express "approver but not initiator" natively: OpenFGA's `define can_approve: approver but not initiator` and SpiceDB's `permission approve = approver - requester`, with conditions or caveats for amount and time ([OpenFGA](https://openfga.dev/docs/configuration-language); [SpiceDB](https://authzed.com/docs/spicedb/concepts/schema)). Cerbos expresses the same as CEL conditions (`R.attr.createdBy != P.id`, `P.attr.approvalLimit >= R.attr.amount`) and since v0.55 fails closed when a DENY rule errors, which is the right default for a bank ([Cerbos conditions](https://docs.cerbos.dev/cerbos/latest/policies/conditions)). Casbin is a library without auditing and without mutually-exclusive role constraints, so it is the wrong fit where decision logs are an audit artefact ([AuthZed on Casbin](https://authzed.com/blog/casbin)). DORA RTS 2024/1774 Art. 21 fixes the staff side: least privilege, segregation of duties "to stop access rights from being combined in ways that could bypass controls", dedicated admin accounts, and access reviews at least yearly (six-monthly for critical functions), so static SoD (initiator and approver roles cannot coexist on one identity) belongs at role-assignment time in the IdP, independent of the runtime check ([Springlex RTS Art. 21](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/dora/rts-rmf-regulation/article-21/)). For KISS, start with an `approvals` table, an in-code policy module with a decision-log table, and adopt Cerbos (attribute rules) or OpenFGA (relationship rules such as joint-account holders and company mandates) when the rule set outgrows amount bands and role checks.

Secrets and tamper evidence have clean free answers. HashiCorp Vault has been BUSL since August 2023 and is now IBM-owned; OpenBao is the MPL-2.0 Linux Foundation fork that added namespaces and shipped 2.6.x and a 2.7.0 beta in 2026 with KMS-backed external keys for Transit and PKI ([OpenBao GitHub](https://github.com/openbao/openbao); [OpenBao 2.7.0](https://openbao.org/community/release-notes/2-7-0/); [WZ-IT](https://wz-it.com/en/blog/openbao-vs-vault-comparison/)). SOPS (MPL-2.0, CNCF sandbox) with age keys covers config at rest ([SOPS](https://github.com/getsops/sops)). immudb is **BSL 1.1** for current versions, so the free tamper-evident audit log is a Postgres `audit_events` table with a per-row SHA-256 hash chain, daily Merkle roots signed by an OpenBao Transit key and anchored to object-locked WORM storage, which also matches DORA RTS Art. 12 logging expectations; Trillian/Rekor is the heavier transparency-log option if external verifiability is wanted ([immudb LICENSE](https://github.com/codenotary/immudb/blob/master/LICENSE); [transparency.dev](https://transparency.dev/verifiable-data-structures/); [Springlex Art. 12](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/dora/rts-rmf-regulation/article-12/)). The fraud baseline for late 2026 is Verification of Payee on every credit transfer (euro-area since 9 October 2025), device binding with Play Integrity at `MEETS_DEVICE_INTEGRITY` or stronger (basic integrity is bypassable by maintained root-hiding tools), risk-tiered SCA using the TRA exemption bands (€100/€250/€500 at 0.13%/0.06%/0.01% fraud rates), a UK-style configurable hold of up to 72 hours, and new-payee friction as bank policy; no EU or UK rule mandating a cooling-off period was found ([Deutsche Bank on VoP](https://corporates.db.com/in-focus/Focus-topics/instant-payments-vop); [Play Integrity](https://developer.android.com/google/play/integrity/overview); [EBA Q&A 2018_4034](https://www.eba.europa.eu/single-rule-book-qa/qna/view/publicId/2018_4034); [Finextra on 72h](https://finextra.com/newsarticle/44831/uk-payments-could-be-delayed-up-to-three-days-to-prevent-fraud)).

## Instant payments and passkeys are table stakes; agent mandates and provable reconciliation are the white space

The feature landscape sorts cleanly once mandates and shipping dates are lined up. Euro-area PSPs had to offer Verification of Payee from 9 October 2025 and send instant transfers at no higher price than standard ones; the EPC VoP rulebook took effect on 5 October 2025; Swift's CBPR+ coexistence for payment instructions ended 22 November 2025 and unstructured-only addresses stop being accepted from November 2026 ([ECB IPR page](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/paym/retail/instant_payments/html/instant_payments_regulation.ht.html); [Finextra](https://www.finextra.com/the-long-read/1471/europes-instant-payments-regulation-and-the-9-october-deadline-explained); [Swift](https://www.swift.com/iso-20022-financial-institutions)). The UK's commercial VRP scheme went live on 2 June 2026 covering ~75% of current accounts, and UK open banking passed one billion payments ([Finexer](https://blog.finexer.com/?p=35851); [OBL](https://www.openbanking.org.uk/insights/2026-a-landmark-year-for-open-banking/)). On the AI side, Starling Assistant (20 March 2026) and Revolut AIR (9 April 2026) execute actions such as creating savings spaces and scheduling transfers, bunq's Finn runs multi-agent logic inside the core with roughly 80% automated support resolution, and Visa Intelligent Commerce Connect reached GA in June 2026 supporting UCP, TAP, MPP and ACP while Mastercard Agent Pay reached all US cardholders in November 2025; the protocols "are not interoperable" ([TNW](https://thenextweb.com/news/starling-assistant-agentic-ai-financial-assistant-uk); [Anthropic bunq case](https://claude.com/customers/bunq); [Trending Topics](https://www.trendingtopics.eu/visa-bets-on-open-infrastructure-as-payment-giants-race-for-agentic-commerce/); [WeTheFlywheel](https://wetheflywheel.com/en/agentic-commerce/acp-vs-ap2/)). Two hard deadlines shape the differentiators: banks must accept the EU Digital Identity Wallet for SCA by **24 December 2027** under eIDAS 2.0 Art. 5f, and the AI Act's high-risk obligations for credit scoring were deferred by the Digital Omnibus to **2 December 2027** ([fintech.global](https://fintech.global/2026/09/04/what-banks-need-to-know-before-the-eudi-wallet-deadline/); [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/eu-digital-omnibus-on-ai-enters-into-force/1825722)). Tokenised deposits are an institutional story (a TCH big-bank network targeted for early 2027, a UK programmable-sterling pilot to end-2026), and the ECB says digital-euro issuance is not before 2029 even if legislation lands in 2026 ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/403701/jpmorgan-citi-major-banks-tokenized-deposit-network); [Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/ecb-digital-euro-could-launch-2029)).

Two inferences matter for scope. First, a rules engine on the ledger ("when salary lands, sweep X to pot Y; cap merchant Z at €50/month; hold until delivery confirmed") delivers most of the user value of "programmable money" without any tokenisation and can later be mapped onto tokenised-deposit conditional payments; it is differentiating now while the blockchain form is speculative. Second, McKinsey's warning that customer-side "financial agents" could erode up to 9% (~$170B) of banking profits is the opening for a transparent bank to build the optimiser in rather than be disintermediated by it, and an open-source bank is uniquely placed to be the "agent-native" account with per-agent spend caps, merchant allowlists and time windows exposed as a first-class API ([TechRepublic](https://www.techrepublic.com/article/news-bank-profit-decline-ai/)). No licensed neobank publishes its core code; Fineract and Brankas Open are cores used by banks, not banks that are open, so publishing the bank's own code, fee logic, fraud-rule categories and model cards is a genuinely unoccupied position ([Intelligrow on Fineract](https://www.intelligrow.co/blog/apache-fineract-architecture)).

## A web core in a Capacitor shell is the thinnest compliant client

iOS 26 made every Home Screen site open as a web app by default, with web push (including declarative push without a service worker), badging, WebAuthn, camera, the Digital Credentials API and OPFS writable streams, so the day-to-day banking UI is feasible as a PWA ([WebKit Safari 26](https://webkit.org/blog/17333/webkit-features-in-safari-26-0/); [firt.dev](https://firt.dev/notes/pwa-ios/)). What a PWA still cannot do is decisive for a regulated bank: no Web NFC, no background sync, no App Store listing, and above all no hardware attestation and no verifiable device-bound key, which is what the EBA opinion requires for the possession element ([EBA Q&A 2019_4827](https://www.eba.europa.eu/single-rule-book-qa/qna/view/publicId/2019_4827)). Apple additionally requires that money-management apps be "submitted by the financial institution performing such services" with the necessary licences, offers in-app account deletion, and rejects "repackaged website" apps under guideline 4.2; Google Play requires a Financial Features declaration and its Integrity API distinguishes `MEETS_STRONG_INTEGRITY` (hardware-backed, Android 13+) from basic integrity, with a default quota of 10,000 requests per day ([Apple guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/); [Google Play Financial Services](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9876821?hl=en); [Play Integrity](https://developer.android.com/google/play/integrity/overview)). Adding native biometrics, App Attest, push and offline cache is exactly what makes a wrapper "app-like" and exactly what the bank needs anyway.

Among the shells, Capacitor has the fewest moving parts: one WKWebView or Android WebView, a JS bridge, and small MIT/Apache community plugins that map directly to Keychain/Keystore, biometric prompts and native certificate pinning (`@capgo/capacitor-native-biometric`, `@capgo/capacitor-ssl-pinning`, `@aparajita/capacitor-secure-storage`), while Ionic's paid Identity Vault is avoidable ([capacitor-ssl-pinning](https://github.com/Cap-go/capacitor-ssl-pinning/blob/main/README.md); [capacitor-native-biometric](https://www.npmjs.com/package/@capgo/capacitor-native-biometric)). Tauri 2 mobile has been stable since October 2024 and patched through 2.12.0 (September 2026), but it has "no supported way to embed native UI components alongside the webview", a thinner banking-plugin ecosystem, and requires a Rust toolchain, which the sandbox cannot satisfy because crates.io is blocked ([Tauri 2.12.0](https://v2.tauri.app/release/tauri/v2.12.0/); [tauri PR #14484](https://github.com/tauri-apps/tauri/pull/14484)). Expo SDK 56 with DOM components makes an RN shell around web screens possible, but Software Mansion's July 2026 benchmark put the RN Android download at 15.8 MB versus 2.0 MB for KMP with 232 MB versus 120 MB resident memory, which is a heavy price for a shell whose job is small ([Expo SDK 56](https://expo.dev/changelog/sdk-56); [Software Mansion](https://swmansion.com/blog/we-built-the-same-app-in-kmp-and-react-native-here-s-what-we-found/)). Kotlin Multiplatform with Compose (iOS stable since 1.8) is the right answer only for a native-first team and offers no web reuse ([JetBrains](https://blog.jetbrains.com/kotlin/2026/01/compose-multiplatform-1-10-0/)). Every large neobank (Monzo, Revolut, N26, Wise) ships native Swift and Kotlin, Nubank runs Flutter with server-driven UI for 100M+ customers, and no neobank was found shipping a WebView-first client, so a web core is a pioneering choice with higher review risk that is nevertheless the only single-codebase option a small team can sustain ([Building Nubank](https://building.nu.com/scaling-with-flutter/); [Monzo iOS posting](https://jobs.accel.com/companies/monzo/jobs/2516475-ios-engineer)).

For the web layer, Svelte 5, Solid, Preact and Lit all land in the 3–8 kB gzipped class against ~42–58 kB for React+ReactDOM; Solid has had the highest State of JS satisfaction for five years running, and React remains the hiring default and the backbone of the richest accessible-component kits ([State of JS 2025](https://2025.stateofjs.com/en-US/libraries/front-end-frameworks/); [framework size analysis](https://github.com/gautemo/frontend-framework-size-analysis)). Material Web is "in maintenance mode pending new maintainers" and should not be a foundation ([material-web](https://github.com/material-components/material-web)). The European Accessibility Act has applied to consumer banking since 28 June 2025 with no grandfathering, EN 301 549's revision moves to WCAG 2.2 AA, and WCAG 3.3.8 Accessible Authentication forbids cognitive tests such as transcribing codes without an alternative, which is one more argument for passkey-first login ([accessible.org](https://accessible.org/online-banking-accessibilityeaa/); [accessscan](https://accessscan.app/guides/eaa-compliance-for-banking)). The KISS choice is **Svelte 5 (or Lit if the team prefers zero compiler magic) with an in-house token set and native HTML semantics**, Preact with `preact/compat` as the pragmatic middle path if Radix-style primitives are wanted. The secure-client pattern is fixed regardless of framework: the native layer owns a hardware-bound key pair, Play Integrity/App Attest tokens, biometric gating, FLAG_SECURE on Android, and pinning; the web layer never holds long-lived secrets and receives short-lived tokens through the bridge only after native authentication, following the documented WebView token-delivery pattern ([Android secure activities](https://developer.android.com/security/fraud-prevention/activities); [BVNK](https://docs.bvnk.com/bvnk/use-cases/cards/display-card-details-webview/); [OWASP MASVS-RESILIENCE](https://mas.owasp.org/MASVS/controls/MASVS-RESILIENCE-1/)). Banks do not do local-first money movement; offline payments exist only in merchant-terminal SDKs with hard caps and 24-hour expiry, so the client should be "offline-readable, online-transactional": an encrypted local cache with per-record `sync_status`, queued non-monetary intents, and fresh SCA on every submission; ElectricSQL (Apache-2.0, now at Neon with everything open-sourced staying open) is the fit for server-authoritative read sync if needed, while PowerSync's FSL server fails the OSS constraint ([Square offline](https://developer.squareup.com/docs/mobile-payments-sdk/android/offline-payments); [Electric joins Neon](https://neon.com/blog/electric-joins-neon)). Test with Playwright (Apache-2.0) for all web flows and Maestro CLI (Apache-2.0) for the native shell, since Playwright cannot drive biometric prompts ([Maestro](https://github.com/mobile-dev-inc/Maestro)).

## Go, Postgres, River and Compose carry the backend; most licences are clean

Production evidence does not crown a bank language: Monzo is "built largely in Go" with ~1,600 microservices and cites the Go compatibility guarantee for painless upgrades; Wise runs 600–700 JVM services in Java and Kotlin; Nubank runs 2.5 million lines of Clojure and bought Cognitect; Mercury chose Haskell for correctness but admits parts are "slower to get stuff ramped up" ([Monzo AMA](https://community.monzo.com/t/monzonaut-ama-bee-platform-engineer/120012?page=2); [Wise 2025](https://medium.com/wise-engineering/wise-tech-stack-2025-update-d0e63fe718c7); [InfoQ Nubank](https://www.infoq.com/podcasts/lucas-cavalcanti-nubank-fintech-clojure/); [Serokell Mercury](https://serokell.io/blog/haskell-in-production-mercury)). For a two-to-six person team that wants a single static binary and no runtime to operate, Go is the lowest-surface choice, with shopspring/decimal (MIT) or, better, BIGINT minor units for money; TypeScript is viable only with integer minor units because the TC39 Decimal proposal is still Stage 1 ([Go 1.26 notes](https://go.dev/doc/go1.26); [shopspring/decimal](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/shopspring/decimal/HEAD/LICENSE); [tc39/proposal-decimal](https://github.com/tc39/proposal-decimal)). Go 1.26 is current but the sandbox has 1.24, so pin `go 1.24` in `go.mod` and avoid 1.25+ features; Node 22 is sufficient for the Svelte build and Capacitor CLI even though Node 24 is the active LTS line ([dev.to runtime comparison](https://dev.to/mr_manushukla/deno-vs-bun-vs-nodejs-in-2026-the-production-backend-runtime-decision-30pc)).

PostgreSQL covers every ledger need. PG17 added logical-replication failover slots and incremental backup, PG18 (GA 25 September 2025) added asynchronous I/O, `uuidv7()`, temporal constraints and data checksums by default; SERIALIZABLE isolation emulates serial execution without extra blocking and always signals conflicts as SQLSTATE `40001`, so the ledger service needs a generic retry wrapper ([PG17 press kit](https://www.postgresql.org/about/press/presskit17/); [PG18 press kit](https://www.postgresql.org/about/press/presskit18/); [transaction isolation](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/current/transaction-iso.html)). HA and backup are entirely free: Patroni (MIT), pgBackRest (MIT), CloudNativePG (Apache-2.0, CNCF sandbox) ([Patroni](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/patroni/patroni/HEAD/LICENSE); [pgBackRest](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/pgbackrest/pgbackrest/HEAD/LICENSE); [CNPG](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cloudnative-pg/cloudnative-pg/HEAD/LICENSE)). Event sourcing should also live in Postgres: KurrentDB (ex-EventStoreDB) is under the Kurrent License v1 with Kafka connectors and encryption-at-rest behind licence keys, so an append-only `events` table plus a transactional outbox is the free design ([KurrentDB LICENSE](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/kurrent-io/KurrentDB/master/LICENSE.md)).

The smallest payments-orchestration stack is Postgres-as-queue: River (MPL-2.0, Go) completes jobs in the same transaction as the ledger write, supports unique and scheduled jobs and wakes workers via LISTEN/NOTIFY, giving exactly-once effects that no external broker can ([River](https://github.com/riverqueue/river)). Sagas should be explicit state machines in a `payment_attempts` table. NATS JetStream is the free "scale later" broker, confirmed Apache-2.0 after the May 2025 CNCF settlement that keeps NATS in the CNCF and assigns the trademarks to the Linux Foundation; Kafka 4.x is free but heavy; Redpanda Community is BSL with audit logging, RBAC and OIDC enterprise-gated, which rules it out for a bank ([CNCF announcement](https://www.cncf.io/announcements/2025/05/01/cncf-and-synadia-align-on-securing-the-future-of-the-nats-io-project/); [Redpanda licensing](https://docs.redpanda.com/current/get-started/licensing/overview/)). For durable execution, Temporal is MIT but a four-service cluster with sharding fixed at first boot; DBOS Transact is MIT and Postgres-only but a Python/TypeScript library; Restate's server is BSL 1.1 and Inngest's server is SSPL ([Temporal LICENSE](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/temporalio/temporal/HEAD/LICENSE); [Restate LICENSE](https://github.com/restatedev/restate/blob/main/LICENSE); [Inngest](https://github.com/Percepta-Core/inngest)). Platform: Docker Compose on one or two VMs with Caddy (Apache-2.0) is the smallest thing that works, k3s (Apache-2.0) the scale path, Nomad is BSL 1.1 since August 2023; Headscale (BSD-3) for operator access; OpenTelemetry Collector into Grafana LGTM (AGPLv3, fine to self-host) or HyperDX (MIT) ([k3s](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/k3s-io/k3s/HEAD/LICENSE); [Nomad LICENSE](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/hashicorp/nomad/HEAD/LICENSE); [Grafana licensing](https://grafana.com/licensing/); [HyperDX](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/hyperdxio/hyperdx/HEAD/LICENSE)). CI/CD: Forgejo (GPLv3) with Forgejo Actions or Woodpecker (Apache-2.0), `just` and `mise`, Renovate, Dagger if build logic outgrows a justfile; Earthly ended active maintenance in April 2025 ([Forgejo](https://codeberg.org/forgejo/forgejo/raw/branch/forgejo/LICENSE); [Earthly shutdown](https://earthly.dev/blog/shutting-down-earthfiles-cloud/)).

The weakest free layer is KYC/AML. Marble is Elastic License 2.0, Hummingbird is paid SaaS, there is no OSS document-verification or liveness tool, and **OpenSanctions bulk data is CC-BY-NC, with the project explicit that "compliance screening is a commercial use even though it generates no revenue"**, so a bank must budget for a paid sanctions-data licence from day one ([Marble LICENSE](https://github.com/checkmarble/marble/blob/main/LICENSE); [OpenSanctions](https://www.opensanctions.org/docs/commercial/exemption/)). Statements and letters are covered by Typst (Apache-2.0) and Gotenberg (MIT); backups by pgBackRest plus restic (BSD-2) to object-locked storage ([Typst](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/typst/typst/HEAD/LICENSE); [restic](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/restic/restic/HEAD/LICENSE)). Hosting is not free beyond a laptop: Fly.io has no free tier for new organisations, Hetzner raised prices twice in 2026 with cost-optimised lines intermittently unorderable, Oracle's Always Free Ampere A1 (4 OCPU/24 GB, capacity permitting) is the only always-on free VM large enough for a demo stack, and an honest staging minimum is roughly **€19–35 per month** ([Fly.io pricing](https://docs.fly.io/about/pricing); [Tom's Hardware on Hetzner](https://www.tomshardware.com/tech-industry/hetzner-to-raise-prices-by-up-to-37-percent-from-april-1); [Oracle free tier guide](https://medium.com/@imvinojanv/setup-always-free-vps-with-4-ocpu-24gb-ram-and-200gb-storage-the-ultimate-oracle-cloud-guide-bed5cbf73d34)).

## Neobank evidence says: compliance controls first, interchange never

The profitable neobanks share three traits: an interest-earning balance sheet, at least one fee line other than card interchange, and very low cost-to-serve. Nubank earned **US$2.9bn net income in FY2025** on 131 million customers at a cost-to-serve of about **US$0.80 per active customer per month**, a figure unchanged since its 2021 F-1 ([Nu FY2025](https://international.nubank.com.br/company/nu-holdings-ltd-reports-fourth-quarter-and-full-year-2025-financial-results/); [Nu F-1](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1691493/000119312521314359/d213207df1.htm)). Revolut reported £1.7bn pre-tax profit on £4.5bn revenue with **76% of turnover fee-based**, subscriptions alone at £708m, and still needed nearly five years from application to a full UK bank licence ([Revolut annual report 2025](https://assets.revolut.com/pdf/annualreport2025.pdf); [HL](https://www.hl.co.uk/news/revolut-reveals-record-profits-in-landmark-year)). Chime, by contrast, derives 72–80% of revenue from interchange-driven payments and posted a US$1.0bn GAAP loss in 2025, while Varo lost about US$92m despite a full charter; US Reg II debit interchange has been capped at 21¢ + 0.05% since 2011 and the cap is under court challenge ([Chime 424B4](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1795586/000162828025030855/chimefinancialinc-finalpro.htm); [American Banker on Varo](https://www.americanbanker.com/news/varo-funding-round-2025q4-call-report); [PayKings](https://paykings.com/blog/debit-interchange-cap-regulation-ii-2026/)). In the EU, the Instant Payments Regulation forces instant transfers to cost no more than standard ones, so transfer fees are not a revenue line either ([Xpollens](https://www.xpollens.com/en/blog/limit-pricing-security-whats-changing-for-instant-payments-in-2025/)). Esh in Israel shows the transparent alternative: zero fees, a 50% share of current-account income, and a real-time display of what the bank earns on each customer's money, run with at most 70 employees ([Calcalist](https://www.calcalistech.com/ctechnews/article/x8ijyku7k)).

The most common thing scaled neobanks got wrong is financial-crime controls lagging growth. The FCA fined Monzo **£21.1m** (July 2025) for onboarding customers with addresses such as Buckingham Palace and breaching a VREQ, Starling **£29m** (October 2024), BaFin fined N26 €9.2m and capped its sign-ups for three years, and the Bank of Lithuania fined Revolut €3.5m, its largest AML penalty ever; Which? found Revolut drew more fraud complaints to the UK Ombudsman than any other firm in 2024 and 2025 ([FCA Monzo](https://www.fca.org.uk/news/press-releases/fca-fines-monzo-21m-failings-financial-crime-controls); [CNBC Starling](https://www.cnbc.com/2025/05/28/goldman-backed-starling-bank-profit-drops-amid-covid-loan-issue.html); [N26](https://n26.com/en-eu/press/press-release/n26-welcomes-bafin-s-lift-of-its-growth-restriction); [Which?](https://www.which.co.uk/news/article/revolut-still-the-worst-uk-firm-for-fraud-complaints-warns-which-axqr43W94wLn)). The second lesson is ledger integrity: Synapse kept the only per-customer sub-ledger against pooled FBO accounts at partner banks, Evolve called those ledgers "materially inaccurate", US$85–96m could not be located, and the FDIC's proposed custodial-account rule would require daily per-beneficial-owner reconciliation but was still not final in the Fall 2025 agenda ([Yale JIA](https://www.yalejournal.org/publications/the-synapse-collapse); [Evolve](https://www.getevolved.com/media-release/evolve-update-on-synapse-reconciliation/); [reginfo.gov](https://www.reginfo.gov/public/do/eAgendaViewRule?pubId=202510&RIN=3064-AG07)). Pre-launch death patterns are also clear: Xinja paid 1.5% on deposits with no lending product, and Volt burned about A$219m building a bespoke ledger before launching products ([Motley Fool AU](https://www.fool.com.au/2021/01/14/does-xinjas-downfall-mean-neobanks-are-dead/); [FinTech Magazine](https://fintechmagazine.com/banking/australian-neo-bank-volt-collapses-following-funding-woes)).

Open source has won in banking only at the edges, as shared standards (FINOS CDM reached Active status in October 2025 with JPMorganChase as a maintainer; FDC3 conformance from BlackRock and Morgan Stanley) or commodity components (Moov ACH, Fineract in 400+ microfinance institutions), and no tier-1 or neobank runs an OSS system of record; Monzo's 176 public repos are tooling, not the ledger ([FINOS CDM](https://www.finos.org/blog/from-standards-to-impact-cdm-becomes-an-active-finos-project); [DPGA Fineract](https://www.digitalpublicgoods.net/r/apache-fineract); [github.com/monzo](https://github.com/monzo)). No regulator has been found objecting to open-source software; what they sanction is weak controls. The proprietary cores' documented pain points, historical-transaction import, API churn and opaque pricing at Mambu and Thought Machine, are exactly where a KISS, append-only, well-documented OSS ledger with first-class import and reconciliation tooling can be a wedge for greenfield licensees ([Gartner Peer Insights](https://www.gartner.com/reviews/market/core-banking-systems/compare/mambu-vs-thought-machine)). The positioning that the evidence supports, in order of strength, is: an Apache-2.0 "bank OS" for greenfield licensees (new digital banks, EMIs, lenders already on Fineract); reconciliation-first "provable ledger" infrastructure with signed daily beneficial-owner attestations; compliance-as-code with testable KYC/AML and VREQ-style policy gates; and a small Esh-pattern income-share reference deployment under a UK/EU EMI licence (€350k capital, roughly 6–15 months) or a US partner bank, used as the demo and the word-of-mouth engine, given N26 reports 73% of new customers arrive by referral ([Binderr EMI guide](https://binderr.com/marketplace/uk/fca-emi-licence-uk-guide); [N26 press](https://n26.com/en-eu/press/press-release/n26-group-reports-first-quarterly-profit-as-growth-in-customer-numbers-accelerates-strongly)). A consumer neobank funded by interchange, a deposit-only high-rate product, a years-long bespoke-ledger build, or a US consumer launch from Europe (Monzo exited after seven years) are the paths to avoid ([Sifted](https://sifted.eu/articles/neobank-us-expansion)).

## Real production data today: zero-credential, free-signup and licensed tiers

The UK Open Banking "Open Data" APIs are the only bank data a project can consume in production today with literally nothing: no key, no registration, no mTLS. OBL's `participant_store.json` lists 20 providers (last updated 2019, still accurate for about 16), and on 4 November 2026 the v2.2 endpoints for ATMs, branches, personal and business current accounts, SME loans, commercial credit cards and FCA service metrics returned HTTP 200 at HSBC, Lloyds, Halifax, Bank of Scotland, NatWest, RBS, Ulster, Nationwide, Danske, Bank of Ireland UK, AIB and First Trust, with Barclays serving ATMs and branches only ([participant_store.json](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/OpenBankingUK/opendata-api-spec-compiled/master/participant_store.json); [Open Data API Dashboard](https://openbanking.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/DZ/pages/1165263140/Open+Data+API+Dashboard)). A working call is `curl -H 'Accept: application/prs.openbanking.opendata.v2.2+json' https://api.hsbc.com/open-banking/v2.2/personal-current-accounts`, which returns 10 products across HSBC UK and first direct; HSBC answers 406 without that Accept header, every live bank still serves v2.2 while v2.3/v2.4 paths 404, responses embed the OBL Open Licence, Barclays sets `cache-control: max-age=28800`, and no bank publishes a numeric rate limit, so fetch a few times a day and cache ([Open Data spec](https://openbankinguk.github.io/opendata-api-docs-pub/); [OBL Open Licence](https://www.openbanking.org.uk/open-licence/)). Santander sits behind an Incapsula bot wall, Metro Bank is mTLS-only, and Monzo and Starling publish no Open Data at all. This is product and reference data (overdraft EARs, fees, locations, service quality), not customer accounts, and it is enough to render real, live comparison and "switching assistant" screens.

Central-bank and treasury feeds complete the zero-credential tier. The ECB Data Portal SDMX REST service and the daily `eurofxref` XML, Frankfurter (open source, self-hostable, 239 currencies, no key), the Bank of Israel `PublicApi/GetExchangeRates` and `GetInterest` endpoints plus its SDMX server at `edge.boi.gov.il` with 43 dataflows (FX, policy rate, Telbor, bank and card interest rates, MASAV ACH, ZAHAV RTGS, TASE securities), the Bank of England IADB CSV export, the Fed H.10 CSV download and the US Treasury FiscalData JSON API ("does not require a user account or registration for a token") all returned 200 on the research date ([ECB API](https://data.ecb.europa.eu/help/api/overview); [frankfurter.dev](https://frankfurter.dev/); [BoI guide](https://boi.org.il/media/kbxlhnvz/שליפת-שערי-חליפין-יציגים-ממאגר-הסדרות-החדש.pdf); [boe R package](https://cran.r-project.org/web/packages/boe/readme/README.html); [FiscalData](https://fiscaldata.treasury.gov/api-documentation/)). Server-side Israeli calls must target only `boi.org.il/PublicApi/*` and `edge.boi.gov.il`, because the BoI HTML site sits behind Radware bot protection.

The free-signup tier has narrowed sharply. GoCardless Bank Account Data, the former Nordigen free PSD2 aggregator that Actual Budget and Firefly III relied on, shows "New signups for Bank Account Data are currently disabled" ([GoCardless](https://bankaccountdata.gocardless.com/new-signups-disabled); [Actual Budget](https://actualbudget.org/docs/advanced/bank-sync/gocardless/)). **Enable Banking's restricted production mode is now the main zero-cost route to real PSD2 data in Europe**: register an app, sign RS256 JWTs with your key, whitelist your own accounts in the portal, and you can read your own balances and transactions across roughly 2,500 banks in 29 countries, with full production gated behind a contract and PIS limited to licensed PISPs ([Enable Banking API](https://enablebanking.com/docs/api/reference/); [Firefly III tutorial](https://docs.firefly-iii.org/tutorials/data-importer/eb/)). Plaid offers a free Trial with up to 10 production Items for US/CA developers who signed up on or after 15 April 2026, while UK/EU production requires becoming an FCA-registered agent of Plaid ([Plaid support](https://support.plaid.com/hc/en-us/articles/16194695660311-Can-I-use-Plaid-for-free); [Plaid FCA blog](https://plaid.com/en-gb/blog/fca-registration-and-how-plaid-can-help/)). Starling and Monzo personal access tokens cover a developer's own account only; Salt Edge advertises a time-limited free Basic tier with conflicting connection counts; FRED and Open Exchange Rates (1,000 requests/month free) need keys; TASE DataHub gives free keys for four basic products at 10 requests per 2 seconds ([docs.monzo.com](https://docs.monzo.com/); [Salt Edge](https://saltedge.com/products/spectre); [OXR](https://openexchangerates.org/signup/free); [tasepy](https://pypi.org/project/tasepy/)).

Everything that touches a third party's customer accounts is licensed. In the UK, OBUK Read/Write v4.0 made FAPI 1.0 Advanced Final mandatory and v4.0.1 Update 1 shipped in September 2026; access requires FCA AISP/PISP authorisation with OBWAC/OBSeal or eIDAS QWAC/QSEAL certificates, or FCA registration as an agent of a licensed AISP, and "an agent ... is not permitted to offer any additional or standalone account information ... outside of its principal's permitted activities" ([OBL security profiles](https://standards.openbanking.org.uk/security-profiles/); [read-write-api-specs releases](https://github.com/OpenBankingUK/read-write-api-specs/releases); [Token.io](https://support.token.io/hc/en-us/articles/30231084155417-Do-I-need-to-have-an-AISP-or-PISP-license-to-use-your-service)). In the EU the Berlin Group NextGenPSD2 1.3.16 (27 November 2025) and openFinance V2 OpenAPI files are free downloads under CC-BY-ND, but production access needs NCA authorisation and eIDAS certificates ([GitLab nextgenpsd2](https://gitlab.com/the-berlin-group/nextgenpsd2); [Berlin Group](https://www.berlin-group.org/open-finance)). Israel adopted the Berlin Group standard via Bank of Israel Directive 368, extended to payment companies by an ISA directive finalised on 7 June 2026, and only entities licensed under the Financial Information Service Law 2021 or the Payment Services Law 2023 may access it; no European aggregator covers Israeli banks, so there is no hobbyist route ([Herzog Fox & Neeman](https://herzoglaw.co.il/en/news-and-insights/israel-securities-authority-publishes-directive-for-payment-companies-regarding-the-implementation-of-an-open-banking-standard/); [Gornitzky](https://www.gornitzky.com/the-open-banking-reform/)). Tooling-wise, Open Bank Project's sandbox is keyless for discovery (227 synthetic banks) but AGPL and synthetic; the OBUK account-info OpenAPI (578 KB) and Berlin Group YAMLs make codegen with oapi-codegen the pragmatic client strategy, since the only maintained OSS clients found are a Go OBIE client (MIT, April 2026) and TypeScript GoCardless SDKs ([OBP README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/OpenBankProject/OBP-API/develop/README.md); [obie-client-go](https://pkg.go.dev/github.com/iamkanishka/obie-client-go)).

## Recommended architecture and build order for a small team

The architecture that follows from the evidence is a deliberately boring core wrapped in a few deliberately novel features. One PostgreSQL cluster holds three schemas: `ledger` (accounts, transactions, entries, holds, period closes, enforced by deferrable triggers and unique idempotency keys), `control` (customers, products, devices, consents, mandates, approvals, decision log, audit chain) and `jobs` (River tables). One Go binary serves the customer API, the back-office API, the River workers and the open-data ingestion; it is split only when a second deploy unit is justified by an ops owner. The customer-auth service is a package inside that binary (WebAuthn, sessions, device attestation, payment signing); Keycloak is deployed only when staff SSO or a FAPI authorization server is needed. Authorization starts as an in-code policy module writing to a decision log and moves to Cerbos or OpenFGA when rules become relational. Secrets come from OpenBao (dev mode locally, single-node Raft in staging) and SOPS-encrypted config in Git. Audit events form a hash chain with daily signed Merkle roots pushed to object-locked storage, and the same mechanism publishes a daily beneficial-owner reconciliation attestation, which is the "provable ledger" differentiator. The web client is Svelte 5 with an in-house token set, wrapped by Capacitor with five plugins (biometric, secure storage, pinning, integrity, push); it caches read models locally with per-record sync status and submits every monetary action online with fresh SCA. Observability is OTel to HyperDX; the platform is Compose behind Caddy on two VMs, graduating to k3s with CNPG when a third node arrives.

The sandbox constraints shape phase ordering rather than the design. With no Docker daemon, the development loop is a local PostgreSQL 16, `go build` and `npm run build`, which fits the Go-plus-Postgres core and the web build exactly and excludes anything container-first (Compose stacks, Keycloak, HyperDX/ClickHouse, Fineract, Blnk's Redis) until a staging host exists. Go 1.24 excludes Formance v3 (Go 1.27) and should be the `go.mod` floor. Node 22 runs the Svelte toolchain and the Capacitor CLI, but native builds need Xcode and the Android SDK, so the mobile shell is a staging-host task and the sandbox tests the web core with Playwright. Blocked crates.io removes Tauri and any Rust tooling from consideration. PostgreSQL 16 lacks `uuidv7()` and temporal constraints, so IDs are generated in Go and effective-date overlap checks live in application code, with a migration note for PG18. Nothing in the zero-credential data tier needs more than outbound HTTPS, so real production data can be on screen in the first week.

Three risks deserve explicit tracking. The regulatory texts are moving: PSR and PSD3 are expected in the Official Journal in Q4 2026 with directly applicable SCA and VoP-for-all-transfers rules, the EU Digital Fairness Act proposal is expected the same quarter, and FiDA was still in trilogue, so the consent store and payment flows should be designed to the compromise texts now ([Worldline](https://worldline.com/en/home/main-navigation/resources/blogs/2026/the-scope-and-timeline-are-locked-in-for-psd3-and-psr-what-should-psps-know); [Projective on FiDA](https://www.projectivegroup.com/fida-back-in-motion-from-ambitious-to-workable/)). Several licences were verified only from secondary sources (Formance's LICENSE file was unreachable, OpenBao's own LICENSE and Novu's were not fetched), so a licence-register check belongs in phase 0. And the web-core client is a pioneering choice no scaled bank has validated, so App Store review and SCA device-binding evidence should be rehearsed on a real device before the architecture is locked.

## Conclusion

The surprising result of this research is how little of a 2026 digital bank needs to be exotic. The correctness properties that make TigerBeetle remarkable are a few hundred lines of carefully tested SQL away in PostgreSQL at the volumes any new bank will see for years, the fraud and SCA controls regulators actually test are policy and workflow rather than cryptographic novelty, and the licence landscape has resolved cleanly into "Apache/MIT/MPL/PostgreSQL and fine" versus "BSL/ELv2/SSPL and out", with the one unavoidable paid item being sanctions data. What has changed since the first neobank wave is that the moat moved from the ledger to the controls around it: every scaled challenger was fined for financial-crime failures, the one systemic collapse (Synapse) was a reconciliation failure, and the pending FDIC rule and PSR both push toward provable daily balances and explainable fraud decisions. A small team that builds those controls as code, publishes them, and anchors a signed reconciliation every day is doing something no incumbent or neobank currently does.

The second implication is that the free, zero-credential data tier is larger than expected and immediately useful. Twelve UK banks' product catalogues, four central banks and the US Treasury can be on a live screen before any licence conversation, and Enable Banking's restricted mode turns the developer's own accounts into real test data. That makes the recommended build order self-funding in credibility: the ledger and the open-data screens prove the stack within weeks, the maker-checker and auth layers prove the controls, and only then does the project need a licensee, an EMI application or a sponsor bank. The agentic layer, the agent-mandate API and EUDI onboarding are where the project should aim to be first, but they sit on top of a core whose chief virtue is that there is nothing surprising in it.
