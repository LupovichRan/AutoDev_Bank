## Key Question 1 — Passkeys / WebAuthn / FIDO2 as primary customer authentication in banking (2026)

### Takeaway
Passkeys are production reality in US, Australian and some Asian banks and are rolling out in Europe (Revolut, Finom, ABANCA), but EU regulators have still issued no passkey-specific SCA statement; the governing text remains the EBA's 2019 opinion on SCA elements, which accepts device-bound keys/apps as "possession" and biometrics (including behavioural) as "inherence" while leaving cloud-synced passkeys unaddressed. WebAuthn Level 3 became a W3C Recommendation on 25 Aug 2026, and PSD3/PSR (final texts agreed April–May 2026, OJ publication expected Q4 2026) will tighten SCA toward phishing resistance but is not yet law.

### Cited Findings

**Standards status**
- WebAuthn Level 3 ("Web Authentication: An API for accessing Public Key Credentials Level 3") became a W3C Recommendation on 25 August 2026; future features go into Level 4 — [W3C news](https://www.w3.org/news/2026/web-authentication-an-api-for-accessing-public-key-credentials-level-3-is-now-a-w3c-recommendation/); [W3C TR page](https://www.w3.org/tr/webauthn/); [FIDO Alliance](https://fidoalliance.org/webauthn-level-3-is-now-a-w3c-recommendation/)
- Level 3 substance unchanged from the 26 May 2026 Candidate Recommendation Snapshot; notable additions: Related Origin Requests (one passkey across up to five or more brand domains), AAGUID no longer zeroed in attested credential data when attestation preference is `none`; Level 3 does not make passkeys more phishing-resistant than Level 2 — [startwithidentity](https://startwithidentity.com/blog/2026-08-31-webauthn-level-3-becomes-a-w3c-recommendation/); [ssojet](https://ssojet.com/blog/webauthn-level-3-changes-for-passkey-implementers); [ppc.land](https://ppc.land/webauthn-level-3-lets-one-passkey-cover-at-least-five-related-domains/)

**Real bank adoption (list compiled by a passkey vendor; status labels are the vendor's)**
- US institutions labelled "fully implemented": Chime, Chase (rollout early 2026), DCU, Fidelity (synced + device-bound options, fallback via app authenticator/push), Merrill Edge/Lynch (synced passkeys plus up to two FIDO2 USB keys, on Bank of America's platform), U.S. Bank (since Nov 2025), Wells Fargo (synced via cloud password manager; iOS 16+/Android 14+), Capital One, American Express (web only, not in app), Truist (web, QR cross-device), PenFed, and ~12 credit unions; Nuvision CU uses passkeys for login *and* transaction approval — [Corbado banking-passkeys FAQ](https://www.corbado.com/faq/banking-passkeys)
- Non-US: Ubank (NAB, Australia) passkey-first app login since mid-2024 **[older]**, NAB goal to phase out passwords within five years (announced Dec 2024); ANZ Plus announced May 2025 (go-live unconfirmed); EQ Bank and ATB Financial (Canada); Revolut (Europe, "limited availability"); Finom (EU fintech, mobile-device logins); ABANCA (Spain) uses passkeys as transaction-authorisation second factor, not primary login (FIDO Paris 2024) **[older]**; SBI Sumishin Net Bank (Japan) login + transaction auth since 2020 **[older]**; Cathay Financial (Taiwan) transaction auth (2025); Chinabank (Philippines) passkeys mandatory for transaction approval after March 2026, replacing SMS OTP — [Corbado banking-passkeys FAQ](https://www.corbado.com/faq/banking-passkeys)
- Not offering passkey login per the same source: Bank of America (U2F second factor only), Citibank, PNC, Citizens, Westpac, HSBC, CommBank, RBC, TD, Scotiabank, BMO, CIBC, National Bank of Canada — [Corbado banking-passkeys FAQ](https://www.corbado.com/faq/banking-passkeys)
- Revolut rolled out passkeys quietly for Personal and Business; third-party analysis found the Personal passkey API returning HTTP 403 (phased rollout), rpId set to `sso.revolut.com`, no Conditional UI (username still required), and Android `assetlinks.json` not at the expected location — [Corbado Revolut analysis](https://www.corbado.com/blog/revolut-passkeys); [Authsignal](https://www.authsignal.com/blog/articles/revoluts-passkey-implementation)
- Monzo: no confirmed passkey login launch found; only a community feature request — [Monzo Community](https://community.monzo.com/t/passkey-login/170278). Nubank: nothing found on passkeys.
- No adoption-rate statistics (enrolment %, login share) were found in any source.

**Regulatory acceptance as SCA (EU)**
- EBA Opinion EBA-Op-2019-06 (June 2019) **[older, still the governing text]**: para 18 — inherence "includes biological and behavioural biometrics"; para 26 — "approaches relying on mobile apps, web browsers or the exchange of (public and private) keys may also be evidence of possession, provided that they include a device-binding process that ensures a unique connection between the PSU's app, browser or key and the device … an app or web browser that does not ensure a unique connection with a device would not be a compliant possession element"; para 27 — a digital signature generated using a private key can evidence possession; Table 2 lists "App or browser with possession evidenced by device binding — such as through a security chip embedded into a device or private key linking an app to a device, or the registration of the web browser linking a browser to a device" as compliant; para 38 — elements must be independent (RTS Art. 9); para 39 — "a digital signature generated by a key, if the key requires a knowledge element to be used for the key to be accessed" can constitute two elements; para 42 — "device binding to an app … in combination with a knowledge or inherence element" is compliant; para 44 — two elements from the same category are not compliant — [EBA Opinion PDF](https://www.eba.europa.eu/sites/default/files/documents/10180/2622242/4bf4e536-69a5-44a5-a685-de42e292ef78/EBA%20Opinion%20on%20SCA%20elements%20under%20PSD2%20.pdf)
- The EBA has made no specific statement on passkeys; no Q&A, opinion or technical standard mentions passkeys, FIDO or WebAuthn — [Corbado EBA Q&A FAQ](https://www.corbado.com/faq/eba-single-rulebook-sca-clarifications) (vendor); confirmed by absence in all searches run.
- Whether a cloud-synced passkey satisfies the device-binding requirement is unsettled; vendor views differ (Thales: regulated FS must "raise the bar" before using passkeys for PSD2 SCA; Corbado: both device-bound and synced can be compliant but each institution owns its SCA risk assessment) — [Thales](https://cpl.thalesgroup.com/blog/access-management/passkeys-future-digital-services); [Corbado](https://www.corbado.com/blog/passkeys-sca-compliance)
- Dynamic linking (RTS Art. 5) must bind amount + payee to the authentication code; a passkey login alone does not satisfy it — the WebAuthn challenge/transaction must carry the payment data — [Security Boulevard, Jul 2026](https://securityboulevard.com/2026/07/passkeys-and-psd2-sca-dynamic-linking-with-webauthn/); [Corbado payer-awareness](https://www.corbado.com/blog/biometrics-payer-awareness); EBA para 37 notes dynamic linking "is typically produced based on the possession element" — [EBA Opinion](https://www.eba.europa.eu/sites/default/files/documents/10180/2622242/4bf4e536-69a5-44a5-a685-de42e292ef78/EBA%20Opinion%20on%20SCA%20elements%20under%20PSD2%20.pdf)
- EBA Q&A 4141 (cited in opinion para 40): an element used for login SCA may be reused in the same session for payment SCA if the other element is performed at payment initiation and dynamic linking is tied to it — [EBA Opinion](https://www.eba.europa.eu/sites/default/files/documents/10180/2622242/4bf4e536-69a5-44a5-a685-de42e292ef78/EBA%20Opinion%20on%20SCA%20elements%20under%20PSD2%20.pdf)
- NIST SP 800-63-4 (final July 2025) is the US reference for AAL levels; FIDO's moderate-assurance whitepaper maps device-bound vs synced passkeys onto NIST AAL1–3 — [NIST CSRC](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/63/4/final); [FIDO whitepaper](https://fidoalliance.org/white-paper-fido-authentication-for-moderate-assurance-use-cases/)

**Device-bound vs synced and recovery**
- FIDO Alliance: device-bound passkeys "recommended for user accounts that require the highest level of protection and assurance"; synced passkeys "can more easily address account recovery challenges" but "create a dependency on the passkey provider and their synchronization fabric"; suggested device-bound recovery pattern is registering a second device-bound passkey with every relying party — [FIDO whitepaper (2023) **[older]**](https://fidoalliance.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/06/FIDO_EDWG-Spring-2023_Paper-4_-Authentication-for-Moderate-Assurance-Use-Cases_FINALv3.docx-1.pdf)
- Academic comparison (Büttner & Gruschka, 2025): security of synced passkeys concentrates in the passkey provider — [arXiv 2501.07380](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.07380v1)
- Fidelity's production recovery pattern: fallback via app authenticator or push notification, or support contact; "extra verification still applies to sensitive transactions" — [Corbado banking-passkeys FAQ](https://www.corbado.com/faq/banking-passkeys)

**PSD3 / PSR (successor regime)**
- Council published final compromise texts on 23 April 2026 (ST 8221/26 PSR, ST 8222/26 PSD3); EP ECON committee approved on 5 May 2026 (PSD3 55–3–5, PSR 50–2–2); plenary vote, Council adoption and OJ publication still pending; OJ publication expected Q4 2026 — [Avanto (Aug 2026)](https://avanto.team/insights/psd3-psr-status); [MoFo (Apr 2026)](https://www.mofo.com/resources/insights/260430-psd3-and-the-payment-services-regulation-key-developments); [PaymentBrief](https://paymentbrief.com/articles/psd3-psr-implementation-operator-checklist-2026/)
- Application timing disputed between sources: Avanto says entry into force 18 months after OJ publication (≈ mid-2028); others say PSR applies 21 months after entry into force with VoP at 27 months; MoFo says "late 2027" with transitional licensing — [Avanto](https://avanto.team/insights/psd3-psr-status); [openbankingtracker](https://www.openbankingtracker.com/guides/psd3-psr-readiness); [MoFo](https://www.mofo.com/resources/insights/260430-psd3-and-the-payment-services-regulation-key-developments)
- PSR content (MoFo summary of final texts): SCA conduct rules move into the directly-applicable PSR and are "more prescriptive"; technical service providers that supply SCA to PSPs must be bound by detailed written agreements; APP fraud treated like an unauthorised transfer with full reimbursement; VoP (name/IBAN match with early warning) extended to all credit transfers not already covered by the Instant Payments Regulation; PSPs must run "risk- and behaviour-based transaction monitoring"; structured fraud information-sharing permitted under GDPR; ICT governance aligned with DORA — [MoFo](https://www.mofo.com/resources/insights/260430-psd3-and-the-payment-services-regulation-key-developments)
- Vendor claims that PSD3/PSR "strengthen … SCA requirements with explicit expectations for phishing-resistant authentication" and increase bank liability where phishing-resistant methods were available but not deployed — [RSA blog](https://www.rsa.com/resources/blog/passwordless/passwordless-banking-fido2-psd3-compliance-post-quantum-security/) (vendor; article text of the PSR not verified, see Gaps)

### Inferences
- A bank designing in late 2026 should treat the 2019 EBA opinion as binding guidance: a passkey counts as the possession element only when the private key is demonstrably bound to the device (platform authenticator with hardware-backed key, attestation checked, or a bank-app-generated non-exportable key). Synced passkeys are a defensible *login* factor paired with a separate knowledge/inherence factor, but for payment SCA + dynamic linking the conservative design is a device-bound credential inside the bank app (or a device-bound passkey with attestation) that signs the transaction payload (amount + payee).
- Production patterns from Fidelity/Merrill/Nuvision suggest the safe UX: passkey-first login, a second registered authenticator (second device-bound passkey or FIDO2 security key) as recovery, step-up for sensitive actions, and support-mediated recovery as last resort.
- Because WebAuthn L3 is final and new work moves to L4, APIs are stable enough to build on without expecting breaking changes; use Related Origin Requests if the bank serves multiple brand domains.
- PSD3/PSR will land in the OJ during the life of this project but will not apply before ~2028; designing for phishing-resistant SCA now is the lowest-regret choice.

### Gaps
- No primary EBA or Commission document on passkeys exists; any statement that "passkeys are SCA-compliant" is vendor interpretation.
- Could not retrieve the PSR final text (ST 8221/26) or an article-level summary of its SCA provisions (phishing resistance, factor independence, accessibility, 180-day AIS re-auth); the two sources attempted returned 403/429. Verify directly on the Council register.
- No engineering-blog material from Monzo, Revolut, Nubank, Wise or Stripe on passkeys or maker-checker was found (search budget exhausted before a targeted follow-up).
- No adoption statistics (passkey enrolment or login share) for any bank.
- FIDO recovery best-practices document was referenced but not retrieved.

## Key Question 2 — Open-source identity providers compared; when a small in-house auth service is preferable

### Takeaway
Keycloak (Apache-2.0, ~37.7k stars) is the only candidate with documented FAPI 2.0 Final + DPoP + passkeys all marked "supported" (since 26.4) and is the default choice for a bank that needs a conformant authorization server; Ory Kratos/Hydra (Apache-2.0) is the best fit when the bank owns its login UI and user store; Zitadel moved to AGPL-3.0 in 2025 and Hanko's backend is AGPL-3.0, which matters for an open-source bank that may offer the stack to others. Authentik, Authelia, SuperTokens and Logto are not FAPI-focused.

### Cited Findings

**License and health (repository pages read 4 Nov 2026)**
- Keycloak: Apache-2.0, ~37.7k stars, Java/Maven; governed under CNCF Code of Conduct with CLOMonitor badge (maturity level not named on page) — [GitHub keycloak/keycloak](https://github.com/keycloak/keycloak)
- Ory Kratos: Apache-2.0, ~13.9k stars, Go; headless; lists passkeys, MFA (TOTP, SMS), account verification and recovery, self-service flows, social sign-in, OIDC, magic links, SAML — [GitHub ory/kratos](https://github.com/ory/kratos). Ory Hydra: Apache-2.0, headless OAuth2/OIDC server with no UI and no user database; you supply a login+consent app; DPoP supported; passkeys delegated to Kratos — [ttlab deep-dive (May 2026)](https://ttlab.hopto.org/blog/culture/2026-05-16-keycloak-authentik-zitadel-ory-sso-oidc-saml-fapi-2026-deep-dive?lang=en)
- Zitadel: license changed from Apache 2.0 to AGPL-3.0 with v3 (announced for 31 March 2025; v3 release reported 2 May 2025); core, Console and Hosted-Login are AGPL-3.0; proto definitions, APIs and SDKs stay Apache 2.0; CLA introduced; commercial licence offered where AGPL doesn't fit — [Zitadel blog](https://zitadel.com/blog/apache-to-agpl); [Zitadel v3 announcement](https://zitadel.com/blog/zitadel-v3-announcement); [GitHub discussion #9529](https://github.com/zitadel/zitadel/discussions/9529); [Zitadel on X](https://x.com/zitadel/status/1902702096660795628). (Note: some 2026 comparison blogs still say Apache 2.0 — they are stale.) Architecture: event-sourcing/CQRS, organisations as tenant unit, passkeys first-class, DPoP out of the box; needs PostgreSQL and event log grows fast — [ttlab](https://ttlab.hopto.org/blog/culture/2026-05-16-keycloak-authentik-zitadel-ory-sso-oidc-saml-fapi-2026-deep-dive?lang=en)
- Authentik: MIT core, code under `authentik/enterprise` under "authentik EE License", ~26.2k stars; SAML, OAuth2/OIDC, LDAP, RADIUS; DPoP stabilised in Dec 2025 release; WebAuthn stage GA — [GitHub goauthentik/authentik](https://github.com/goauthentik/authentik); [ttlab](https://ttlab.hopto.org/blog/culture/2026-05-16-keycloak-authentik-zitadel-ory-sso-oidc-saml-fapi-2026-deep-dive?lang=en); passkey passwordless login documented for 2026.8 — [computingforgeeks](https://computingforgeeks.com/keycloak-vs-authentik-vs-zitadel-vs-authelia/)
- Authelia: Apache-2.0, ~29.5k stars, Go; reverse-proxy SSO/MFA portal; "OpenID Certified" OIDC provider; FIDO2/WebAuthn keys and passkey passwordless — [GitHub authelia/authelia](https://github.com/authelia/authelia); described as not FAPI 2.0 compliant — [ttlab](https://ttlab.hopto.org/blog/culture/2026-05-16-keycloak-authentik-zitadel-ory-sso-oidc-saml-fapi-2026-deep-dive?lang=en); passkeys count as one factor — [computingforgeeks](https://computingforgeeks.com/keycloak-vs-authentik-vs-zitadel-vs-authelia/)
- SuperTokens core: Apache-2.0 except `ee/` directory (separate licence), ~15.3k stars, Java, "open-core"; MFA and multi-tenancy listed; passkeys not listed on the core README — [GitHub supertokens/supertokens-core](https://github.com/supertokens/supertokens-core)
- Logto: MPL-2.0, ~14.8k stars, TypeScript; "full support for OIDC, OAuth 2.1, and SAML", MFA, organisation RBAC; passkeys not mentioned on README — [GitHub logto-io/logto](https://github.com/logto-io/logto)
- Hanko: AGPL-3.0 for the repository/backend; `hanko-elements` and `hanko-frontend-sdk` MIT; ~9.1k stars; Go backend; passwords, passcodes, passkeys, TOTP, security keys, OAuth SSO, custom OIDC, SAML SSO (labelled "Enterprise SSO") — [GitHub teamhanko/hanko](https://github.com/teamhanko/hanko)

**FAPI 2.0 and passkeys per product**

- Keycloak release notes: 26.3 — passkeys integrated in default username forms, simplified WebAuthn/passkey registration; 26.4 — "Passkeys integration: supported", "FAPI 2 Final: supported", "DPoP: supported", federated client authentication (preview); 26.5 — JWT Authorization Grant (preview), MCP authorization server support; 26.6 — JWT Authorization Grant supported, federated client auth supported, new DPoP guide, zero-downtime patch releases supported, Java 25; 26.7 — "Discoverable credential" (required/preferred/discouraged) replaces Yes/No in WebAuthn policy; 26.8 — OpenID4VCI promoted to preview (Lissi and Valera wallet guides), OpenID4VP experimental, client secret rotation supported; 26.8.1 is current — [Keycloak release notes](https://www.keycloak.org/docs/latest/release_notes/index.html)
- Keycloak passed FAPI 2.0 conformance in 2025; ROPC grant disabled by default since v25; Helm sizing example requests 500m CPU / 1Gi RAM with 2Gi limit; Organizations GA since 25 but "not true multi-tenancy"; hundreds of realms slow start — [ttlab](https://ttlab.hopto.org/blog/culture/2026-05-16-keycloak-authentik-zitadel-ory-sso-oidc-saml-fapi-2026-deep-dive?lang=en)
- No source established FAPI 2.0 conformance for Zitadel, Authentik, Ory Hydra, Authelia, SuperTokens, Logto or Hanko — absence across [ttlab](https://ttlab.hopto.org/blog/culture/2026-05-16-keycloak-authentik-zitadel-ory-sso-oidc-saml-fapi-2026-deep-dive?lang=en), [computingforgeeks](https://computingforgeeks.com/keycloak-vs-authentik-vs-zitadel-vs-authelia/), [supertokens blog](https://supertokens.com/blog/sso-providers)
- Pocket ID is cited as a lighter option when passkeys for staff are the only requirement — [wetheflywheel](https://wetheflywheel.com/en/guides/authentik-alternatives-2026/)

**Build vs run an IdP**
- Industry rule of thumb (one 2026 source): below ~300k users SaaS is almost always cheaper; above ~2M users self-hosting almost always is; self-hosting costs include HA, backups, CVE response, 24/7 on-call and audit scope; Ory Hydra "fits when you already own the user database and login UI and only need OIDC/OAuth on top" — [ttlab](https://ttlab.hopto.org/blog/culture/2026-05-16-keycloak-authentik-zitadel-ory-sso-oidc-saml-fapi-2026-deep-dive?lang=en)

### Inferences
- For a bank that must expose FAPI-grade APIs (open banking, third-party access) and wants a certified AS without writing one, Keycloak is the only free candidate with FAPI 2 Final + DPoP + passkeys at "supported" status; Ory Hydra is a credible second if the bank is willing to run conformance tests itself.
- KISS in-house auth is reasonable when the bank's *customer* channel is a first-party app only (no third-party OAuth clients, no open-banking AS obligations): a small service that stores WebAuthn credentials, issues short-lived opaque/JWT sessions and signs transactions is a few thousand lines, avoids a 1–2 GiB Java process per node, and keeps the SCA logic (dynamic linking, device binding) under direct control. The moment the bank needs OAuth clients, federation, FAPI conformance or staff SSO to many internal tools, an IdP pays off.
- A pragmatic split: Keycloak (or Ory) for staff/back-office SSO and open-banking AS; a thin in-house customer-auth service (or Ory Kratos) for the customer channel.
- Licensing: Keycloak/Ory/Authelia/OpenFGA/SpiceDB/Cerbos/OPA/Casbin are all Apache-2.0 — the cleanest set for an open-source bank. AGPL (Zitadel, Hanko backend) is acceptable for a self-hosted bank but is a consideration if the project wants to be embedded by others; MPL-2.0 (Logto, OpenBao, SOPS) is file-level copyleft and generally unproblematic.

- Latest release tags and dates for every project could not be read (GitHub API blocked; release sections empty in fetched pages).
- Ory Keto (Zanzibar-style authz) and Ory Hydra repository pages were not fetched; license assumed Apache-2.0 from the ttlab article only.
- No independent benchmark of memory/CPU footprint across IdPs; only Keycloak's Helm example sizing was found.
- No source on whether Zitadel/Authentik/Logto plan FAPI 2.0 certification.

## Key Question 3 — Authorization models and engines for maker-checker / SoD

### Takeaway
All five engines are Apache-2.0; the Zanzibar-style engines (SpiceDB, OpenFGA) express "approver but not initiator" natively with set exclusion and support amount/time via caveats/conditions, while Cerbos (CEL conditions) and OPA (Rego) express the same rule as attribute comparisons and are easier to pair with amount thresholds and time-locks; Casbin is a library, lacks auditing, and does not support mutually-exclusive role constraints. Maker-checker state (pending/approved, who did what) must live in the application's ledger/workflow store; the authz engine only decides whether a given principal may take the next step.

### Cited Findings

**Engines, licenses, health (pages read 4 Nov 2026)**
- OpenFGA: Apache-2.0, ~6.1k stars, Go, "inspired by Google Zanzibar" — [GitHub openfga/openfga](https://github.com/openfga/openfga)
- SpiceDB: Apache-2.0, ~7.2k stars, Go, Zanzibar-inspired; self-host or AuthZed Cloud; no separate enterprise edition mentioned — [GitHub authzed/spicedb](https://github.com/authzed/spicedb)
- Cerbos PDP: Apache-2.0, ~4.6k stars, Go, "open-core": the PDP is open source, Cerbos Hub is a separate cloud control plane — [GitHub cerbos/cerbos](https://github.com/cerbos/cerbos)
- OPA: Apache-2.0, ~12.4k stars, CNCF **graduated** — [GitHub open-policy-agent/opa](https://github.com/open-policy-agent/opa)
- Casbin: Apache-2.0, ~20.5k stars, Go library (plus ports); models: ACL, RBAC (with domains/tenants, resource roles), ABAC, RESTful, deny-override, priority — [GitHub casbin/casbin](https://github.com/casbin/casbin)

**Expressing "approver must not be the initiator"**
- SpiceDB: exclusion operator `-` computes the set difference; intersection `&` requires both sets; union binds tighter than `&`/`-` so parenthesise. Example (from docs semantics): `definition expense { relation requester: user; relation approver: user; permission approve = approver - requester }`; caveats attach boolean expressions with context parameters to relationships and are evaluated at `CheckPermission`; `use typechecking` validates schema at write time — [SpiceDB schema docs](https://authzed.com/docs/spicedb/concepts/schema)
- OpenFGA: `or` (union), `and` (intersection), `but not` (exclusion, "useful for block lists and exclusions"); conditional relationships "only considered if a specific condition is met". Illustrative model: `type request / relations / define approver: [user] / define initiator: [user] / define can_approve: approver but not initiator` — [OpenFGA configuration language](https://openfga.dev/docs/configuration-language)
- Cerbos: YAML policies with CEL conditions; `P` = `request.principal`, `R` = `request.resource`; `expr: (R.attr.createdBy != P.id)` for SoD; numeric limits e.g. `R.attr.amount <= 1000` and `P.attr.approvalLimit >= R.attr.amount`; time functions `now()`, `timestamp()`, `timeSince()`, `duration("1h")` (units ns…h only, no days); CEL runtime errors make an ALLOW not match and, since v0.55, make a DENY fail closed — [Cerbos conditions docs](https://docs.cerbos.dev/cerbos/latest/policies/conditions)
- OPA: supports static SoD as mutually-exclusive role assignment ("two roles cannot be assigned together"); `graph.reachable()` makes role-hierarchy queries feasible — [comparison gist (2022–23) **[older]**](https://gist.github.com/panchicore/e97e01152ac5819ca5e2627655510eff)
- Casbin: listed as not supporting mutually-exclusive role constraints — [same gist **[older]**](https://gist.github.com/panchicore/e97e01152ac5819ca5e2627655510eff); "lacks built-in auditing tools" — [AuthZed blog (vendor)](https://authzed.com/blog/casbin)
- Model summaries: OpenFGA evaluates whether relationships exist using a model plus stored/contextual tuples; Cerbos evaluates whether an action is allowed from policy plus request-supplied attributes (stateless PDP, no relationship lookups) — [Cerbos migration post](https://www.cerbos.dev/blog/migrating-from-openfga-to-cerbos); [Cerbos comparisons](https://www.cerbos.dev/comparisons)
- Four-eyes definition: a dual-review control where independence matters more than repetition; ties to separating request, approval and execution duties — [nhimg glossary](https://nhimg.org/glossary/four-eyes-principle/)
- No worked maker-checker/four-eyes example was published by any of the five projects as far as searches found — [search summary](https://startwithidentity.com/articles/top-7-open-source-authorization-tools/)

**Regulatory anchor for staff SoD**
- DORA RTS (EU) 2024/1774 Art. 21(1): access rights on "need-to-know, need-to-use and least privilege"; "segregation of duties … to prevent unjustified access to critical data and to stop access rights from being combined in ways that could bypass controls"; generic/shared accounts limited and users identifiable at all times; privileged/emergency/admin access on need-to-use or ad-hoc basis; withdrawal without undue delay; reviews at least yearly (six-monthly for critical/important functions); strong authentication for remote, privileged, critical-function and publicly accessible assets; Art. 21(3): dedicated admin accounts and automated PAM where feasible — [Springlex RTS Art. 21](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/dora/rts-rmf-regulation/article-21/)

### Inferences
- Recommended decomposition: (1) **workflow state** (initiated → pending approval(s) → approved/rejected/executed, with required-approver-count, amount tier, and `not_before` for time-locks) stored transactionally beside the ledger; (2) **authz check** before each transition. For the check, OpenFGA/SpiceDB are natural when approval rights derive from relationships (joint-account holders, company directors/mandates, team hierarchies); Cerbos/OPA are natural when rules are attribute-driven (amount bands, per-user limits, time windows, "approver != initiator"). Either works; OpenFGA with conditions or SpiceDB with caveats covers both if the amount and time are passed as context.
- Time-locked approvals (cooling-off, delayed execution) and "N-of-M" quorum are workflow concerns, not single-check concerns: encode the quorum count and unlock time in the resource, and let the policy check `approvals_count >= required` and `now() >= not_before`.
- Static SoD (role conflicts, e.g. "payment-initiator" and "payment-approver" cannot both be assigned) should be enforced at role-assignment time in the IdP/admin service and periodically re-certified (DORA Art. 21 review cadence), independent of the runtime engine.
- Casbin fits only as an embedded library in a single service; for a bank with audit obligations prefer a PDP with decision logging (OPA decision logs, Cerbos audit logs) or an authz store with relationship history (SpiceDB/OpenFGA).

### Gaps
- No bank/fintech engineering blog on maker-checker implementation (Monzo, Wise, Stripe, Revolut) was retrievable before the search budget ran out.
- The OPA/Casbin SoD comparison is from 2022–23 gists; current Casbin may differ.
- Ory Keto was not evaluated (no fetch).
- Decision-log/audit features of each engine were not verified against docs.

## Key Question 4 — Secrets, keys, signing and tamper-evident logs

### Takeaway
HashiCorp Vault has been BUSL-1.1 (source-available, not OSI) since Aug 2023 and is now IBM-owned, so the free open-source choice is OpenBao (MPL-2.0, Linux Foundation/OpenSSF-hosted fork of Vault 1.14, with namespaces and an active PKCS#11 working group); Infisical core is MIT but its `ee/` directories need a paid licence; SOPS (MPL-2.0, CNCF sandbox) with age keys covers config secrets. For tamper-evident audit/ledger logs, Trillian and Sigstore Rekor are the open transparency-log stack, whereas immudb is **BSL-1.1** and does not meet a strict free-OSS constraint.

### Cited Findings

**Vault vs OpenBao**
- Vault relicensed to Business Source License 1.1 in August 2023; OpenBao forked from Vault 1.14 (last MPL-2.0 release); BUSL permits use/modification but prohibits competing offerings; converts to MPL-2.0 four years after each release — [WZ-IT comparison](https://wz-it.com/en/blog/openbao-vs-vault-comparison/); [OpenLogic](https://www.openlogic.com/blog/hashicorp-vault-alternatives-openbao-vs-vault); [TechTarget (2023, fork origin) **[older]**](https://techtarget.com/searchitoperations/news/366563095/IBM-engineers-hatch-Linux-Foundation-HashiCorp-Vault-fork)
- IBM completed the HashiCorp acquisition in Feb 2025; one source says the Vault licence now names IBM as licensor and Vault 2.0 (April 2026) added IBM licence integration for Enterprise (not verified against primary) — [WZ-IT](https://wz-it.com/en/blog/openbao-vs-vault-comparison/)
- Vault GitHub: ~36.5k stars; licence text not visible on the fetched page — [GitHub hashicorp/vault](https://github.com/hashicorp/vault)
- OpenBao: MPL-2.0, ~8.9k stars; security contact at openssf.org, mailing list on lists.openssf.org, chat on linuxfoundation.zulipchat.com; README commits to OSI-approved licence under open governance; working groups for namespaces, PKCS#11, scalability, supply chain, UI; features: encrypted KV (disk or PostgreSQL), dynamic secrets (AWS, SQL), encrypt/decrypt-as-a-service, leasing/revocation — [GitHub openbao/openbao](https://github.com/openbao/openbao)
- OpenBao API-compatible with Vault 1.14.9 and has added namespaces (Vault Enterprise-only); v2.6.1 released 2026-07-22 (secondary sources) — [youngju.dev release-note analysis](https://www.youngju.dev/split/2026-07-17-openbao-v2-6-vault-divergence); [opentechhub](https://www.opentechhub.io/openbao/)
- Vault Enterprise still cited as better for Vault Secrets Operator, HSM integration and commercial SLAs — [OpenLogic](https://www.openlogic.com/blog/hashicorp-vault-alternatives-openbao-vs-vault)

**Infisical, SOPS, age, SoftHSM**
- Infisical: MIT "with the exception of the `ee` directories" which contain premium features requiring an Infisical licence; ~30.3k stars; lists SSO, approval workflows, PKI, KMS, SSH/PAM without marking which are EE — [GitHub Infisical/infisical](https://github.com/Infisical/infisical); a third-party teardown says 800+ files under `backend/src/ee/` need a licence key that phones home (offline key for air-gapped) — [dev.to teardown (secondary)](https://dev.to/beton/infisical-pricing-teardown-2026-1ang)
- SOPS: MPL-2.0, ~23.6k stars, "a Cloud Native Computing Foundation sandbox project"; backends: AWS KMS, GCP KMS, Azure Key Vault, HuaweiCloud KMS, age, PGP; an `hcvault` folder exists in the tree — [GitHub getsops/sops](https://github.com/getsops/sops)
- SoftHSMv2: software PKCS#11 implementation originating in OpenDNSSEC; ~1.1k stars, 1,583 commits; no maintenance statement and no licence text visible on fetched page — [GitHub softhsm/SoftHSMv2](https://github.com/softhsm/SoftHSMv2)

**Tamper-evident logs / ledger signing**
- Trillian: Merkle-tree verifiable log; every record is a leaf; tree head hashes are signed with a private key; clients verify inclusion proofs back to the signed head; auditors need a history of tree heads stored outside the system — [transparency.dev](https://transparency.dev/verifiable-data-structures/); [certificate-transparency.org](https://certificate-transparency.org/log-proofs-work)
- Rekor (Sigstore): append-only, tamper-evident transparency log built on Trillian; entries verifiable offline via inclusion proofs — [Chainguard](https://www.chainguard.dev/supply-chain-security-101/what-is-sigstore-rekor)
- immudb: **Business Source License 1.1**, ~9k stars, Go; "cryptographic commit log with parallel Merkle Tree"; clients verify via `VerifiableGet`, `immudb_verify_row`, `immudb_verify_tx` — [GitHub codenotary/immudb](https://github.com/codenotary/immudb)
- Design guidance: hash chains prove sequence integrity, Merkle roots give one fingerprint over an ordered set, and tamper-*evidence* (not tamper-proofing) depends on publishing signed checkpoints to an independent location — [designgurus](https://www.designgurus.io/answers/detail/how-do-you-design-tamperevident-audit-logs-merkle-trees-hashing); [arXiv 2306.01886](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.01886)
- DORA RTS 2024/1774 Art. 12 sets logging requirements including access-control and identity-management events; vendors read it as requiring tamper-protected logs — [search summary citing Springlex Art. 12](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/dora/rts-rmf-regulation/article-12/); [idenhq (vendor)](https://www.idenhq.com/en/blog/dora-identity-controls-access-gaps-2026)

### Inferences
- Stack that satisfies "free OSS only": OpenBao (secrets, transit encryption/signing, dynamic DB creds, PKI), SOPS+age for config-at-rest in Git, SoftHSM2 behind OpenBao's PKCS#11 work only for dev/test (treat a real HSM or cloud KMS as a production upgrade path), and a Trillian-backed (or Rekor-style) transparency log for ledger/audit entries with signed tree heads published to a separate store (object storage with WORM, or a second log).
- Avoid immudb if the BSL constraint is strict; if a database-shaped verifiable store is wanted, a Postgres table with a per-row hash chain plus periodic Merkle checkpoint signed via OpenBao transit is the simplest free design and matches DORA's logging/integrity expectations.
- Ledger signing: sign the canonical journal entry (or batch Merkle root) with a key held in OpenBao transit; record key version in the entry; rotate keys with OpenBao's versioned transit keys.

### Gaps
- age (FiloSottile/age) and SoftHSMv2 licence pages were not read (age is widely known as BSD-3-Clause, SoftHSM as BSD-2-Clause, but this was not verified in-session).
- OpenBao's PKCS#11/HSM auto-unseal status and Raft storage status were not confirmed from primary docs (only the working group's existence).
- Vault 2.0 / IBM-licensor claims are from a single secondary source.
- Trillian and Rekor repository licences/maintenance status were not fetched (both are widely known as Apache-2.0; unverified here).

## Key Question 5 — Regulatory and standards baseline (late 2026)

### Takeaway
The binding EU baseline today is PSD2 + RTS 2018/389 (SCA, dynamic linking, TRA exemptions), DORA (applicable since 17 Jan 2025 with RTS 2024/1774 on access control and logging), the Instant Payments Regulation (VoP since 9 Oct 2025), and eIDAS 2.0 (banks must accept the EUDI Wallet by 24 Dec 2027). Standards to build to: FAPI 2.0 Security Profile (final Feb 2025), NIST SP 800-63-4 (final 31 Jul 2025), OWASP ASVS 5.0.0 (30 May 2025), PCI DSS v4.0.1 (all 51 future-dated requirements mandatory since 31 Mar 2025). PSD3/PSR is agreed but not yet in the OJ.

### Cited Findings
- DORA (Regulation (EU) 2022/2554) applies from 17 January 2025; ESAs said no grace period — [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/ireland/financial-services/1573812/the-dora-train-pulls-into-the-station-dora-applies-from-17-january-2025); [NatLawReview](https://natlawreview.com/article/eus-digital-operational-resilience-act-comes-effect); [Central Bank of Ireland DORA page](https://centralbank.ie/regulation/digital-operational-resilience-act-(dora)/communications-and-publications)

- RTS 2024/1774 Art. 21 access-control detail (see Q3) and Art. 20 identity management; Recital 14: uniquely identify individuals and systems; shared accounts only exceptionally with accountability maintained — [Springlex RTS Art. 21](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/dora/rts-rmf-regulation/article-21/)

## Key Question 6 — Fraud and risk controls standard in 2026

### Takeaway
By late 2026 the standard EU/UK control set is: Verification of Payee on every SEPA credit transfer (euro area since 9 Oct 2025), risk- and behaviour-based transaction monitoring (required by PSR; a quarter of banks already use behavioural biometrics), device binding as the SCA possession anchor, TRA-exemption fraud-rate management, UK-style payment delays (up to 72 hours) and mandatory APP-fraud reimbursement (£85k cap), and contextual scam warnings in the payment journey. No regulatory source for a mandated "cooling-off period for new payees" was found.

### Cited Findings
- VoP under the Instant Payments Regulation: euro-area PSPs must offer free name/IBAN verification from 9 October 2025; non-euro member states from 9 July 2027; applies to instant and regular SEPA credit transfers; API-initiated single payments cannot opt out — [Deutsche Bank](https://corporates.db.com/in-focus/Focus-topics/instant-payments-vop); [PwC Legal](https://legal.pwc.de/en/news/articles/verification-of-payee-requirements-vop-under-the-eus-instant-payments-regulation-ipr); [Finextra](https://www.finextra.com/the-long-read/1471/europes-instant-payments-regulation-and-the-9-october-deadline-explained)
- EPC VoP scheme rulebook entered into force 5 October 2025 — [The Paypers](https://thepaypers.com/regulations/news/epcs-verification-of-payee-scheme-rulebook-enters-into-effect)
- PSR (final text) extends VoP to all credit transfers with early-warning on mismatch, requires risk- and behaviour-based transaction monitoring, allows structured fraud-data sharing, and treats APP fraud as reimbursable — [MoFo](https://www.mofo.com/resources/insights/260430-psd3-and-the-payment-services-regulation-key-developments)
- UK: Payment Services (Amendment) Regulations 2024 in force 30 Oct 2024 **[older]** allow PSPs up to 72 hours to investigate payments with reasonable grounds to suspect fraud — [Finextra](https://finextra.com/newsarticle/44831/uk-payments-could-be-delayed-up-to-three-days-to-prevent-fraud); [MoneyWeek](https://moneyweek.com/personal-finance/banks-given-additional-time-to-investigate-suspicious-payments-tackling-financial-fraud)
- UK PSR mandatory APP reimbursement from 7 Oct 2024 **[older]**, cap reduced from £415,000 to £85,000, cost split 50/50 sending/receiving PSP; >99% of claims within cap — [FStech](https://fstech.co.uk/fst/PSR_APP_Reimbursement_Scheme_Comes_Into_Effect.php); [RedCompass](https://redcompasslabs.com/insights/app-fraud-reimbursement-rules-uk-85000)
- Behavioural biometrics: SAS/ACFE 2026 benchmarking found a quarter of surveyed banks use it; positioned as one layer alongside device intelligence and identity signals; BioCatch DeviceIQ (2026) targets emulators, cloaked browsers, jailbroken devices; vendors describe SIM/device binding + behavioural biometrics as complementary — [FinTech Futures](https://www.fintechfutures.com/biometrics-id-verification/why-banks-are-turning-to-behavioural-biometrics-to-help-fight-fraud); [Biometric Update (Mar 2026)](https://www.biometricupdate.com/202603/biocatch-targets-ai-driven-banking-fraud-at-the-device-level-with-deviceiq)
- EBA 2019 opinion para 18 recognises behavioural biometrics as a possible inherence element (quality of implementation decides) — [EBA Opinion](https://www.eba.europa.eu/sites/default/files/documents/10180/2622242/4bf4e536-69a5-44a5-a685-de42e292ef78/EBA%20Opinion%20on%20SCA%20elements%20under%20PSD2%20.pdf)
- Scam warnings: banks test placement, presentation and wording of warnings in payment journeys; deviations from established behaviour indicate account takeover, coercion or scams — [FinTech Futures](https://www.fintechfutures.com/biometrics-id-verification/why-banks-are-turning-to-behavioural-biometrics-to-help-fight-fraud)
- Fraud scale (vendor data): BioCatch says APP fraud exceeds $1 trillion annually; UK social-engineering scams up 62% (2026 report) — [Biometric Update (Oct 2025)](https://www.biometricupdate.com/202510/scams-overtake-1-trillion-as-ai-supercharges-global-fraud-networks-biocatch); [Biometric Update (Apr 2026)](https://www.biometricupdate.com/202604/uk-social-engineering-scams-jump-62-as-fraud-tactics-shift-biocatch)
- TRA exemption thresholds and fraud-rate method — see Q5 ([EBA Q&A 2018_4034](https://www.eba.europa.eu/single-rule-book-qa/qna/view/publicId/2018_4034))
- Chinabank (Philippines) made passkeys mandatory for transaction approval from March 2026, replacing SMS OTP — [Corbado banking-passkeys FAQ](https://www.corbado.com/faq/banking-passkeys)

### Inferences
- Minimum control set for the design: (1) VoP call before every credit transfer with mismatch warning and forced acknowledgement; (2) device registration/binding with attestation and a device-trust score; (3) rules + model-based transaction monitoring with per-customer behavioural baseline; (4) risk-tiered SCA: TRA exemptions for low-value, step-up (device-bound signature with dynamic linking) for new payee / high value / anomalous device; (5) configurable hold/delay (UK-style up to 72 h) with customer notification; (6) new-payee friction as a *bank policy* (first payment limit, delayed execution, extra warning) even though no EU/UK rule was found mandating a cooling-off; (7) scam-warning content keyed to risk signals; (8) fraud-rate reporting pipeline (rolling 90-day, per transaction type) to keep TRA exemptions legal.
- For joint/business accounts, maker-checker is itself a fraud control: a second approver on a different registered device breaks most social-engineering flows.

### Gaps
- No regulatory or industry-standard source for a "cooling-off period for new payees"; only the UK 72-hour investigative delay was documented.
- No primary regulator document on behavioural biometrics or device binding standards beyond the EBA 2019 opinion.
- No data on confirmation-of-payee match-rate performance or false-positive rates.
- UK Confirmation of Payee (Pay.UK) rules were not separately researched.
